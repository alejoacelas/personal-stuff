# Recommendations

Each group says what to buy, based on the Inputs tab. Where your inputs don't narrow the choice, there are options with the trade-off that decides between them. The Questions tab lists the answers that would change these most.

## 1. Free up the room

- **Buy a long ball-end hex key set** to reach the recessed bolts. Take one bolt out first to check the size.
- **Disassemble the bed frame and closet.** Store the panels flat against a wall behind the leaning mattress, so together they take one strip of wall.
- **Keep the mattress upright with a strap** or two bungee cords fixed to the stored panels, so it doesn't slide or sag.
- **Clothes without the closet.** Pick one:
  1. A freestanding clothes rail: easiest to see and reach; takes about 1 m of floor.
  2. Two lidded boxes under the desk: takes no extra floor; clothes stay folded.
  3. Over-door hooks plus one box: cheapest; fits your ~15 garments but looks cluttered.

## 2. Make the floor comfortable

The mattress is the most comfortable floor surface you have: lay it down when you want to lounge. For the rest of the floor, pick one:

1. A large soft rug with a non-slip pad: warmer and looks finished; harder to move.
2. A thick exercise mat (10–15 mm): also serves for calisthenics; rolls away.

## 3. Light

- **Daylight:** a tall mirror beside the window, angled to throw sun into the room. It does what an outside reflector would, without installation.
- **Evening:** one warm floor lamp (2700 K bulb) for the room.
- **Work:** a desk lamp that doesn't glare on the screen, such as a monitor light bar or an adjustable arm lamp.
- **Winter:** you have a JUXLamp sunrise alarm and light therapy lamp in Colombia; bring it on your next trip.

## 4. Desk

- **Before buying a footrest,** crank the TROTTEN down until your elbows are at desk height with your feet flat. You need a footrest only if your feet then don't reach the floor.
- **Multiplug:** a surge-protected strip is enough. Your Anker 67 W charger already handles USB-C.
- **Paper:** one ream of A4 80 gsm.

## 5. Kitchen and dinners

Your 4 plates and 4 bowls seat four people, including you.

- **5 L pot.** Pick one:
  1. Non-stick: easy to clean; the coating wears out in a few years.
  2. Stainless steel: lasts decades; food sticks more.
- **Wok.** Pick one:
  1. Carbon steel, flat bottom: cheap, gets very hot, lasts; needs seasoning and drying.
  2. Non-stick: no upkeep; doesn't sear as well.
- **Knife.** Start with one:
  1. Chinese cleaver: chops vegetables and meat, and scoops food into the pan.
  2. Chef's knife (20 cm): more precise for small cuts; the common default.
- **Egg pan:** a 20 cm non-stick pan. Skip it if you choose a non-stick wok.
- **Cleaning:** hire a cleaner before each dinner, or every two weeks.

## 6. Exercise

- **Pull-up bar.** None of these needs a drill:
  1. Doorframe leverage bar: sturdiest and takes the most weight; needs moulding around the frame and can mark the paint.
  2. Tension bar inside the frame: leaves no marks; can slip under dynamic moves.
  3. Gym rings hung on a park bar: no marks and more exercises; requires going out.
- **Clothes:** two sets of shorts and quick-dry t-shirts, for example from Decathlon.

## 7. Carrying things

- **Always:** a packable tote that folds into your backpack.
- **On the bike.** Pick one:
  1. Rear rack plus one or two panniers: carries the most, and the weight sits low. Needs mounting points on the frame.
  2. Front basket: easy to load and fits any bike; carries less and makes steering heavier.
  3. A bigger roll-top backpack: no bike parts; heavy loads on your back.
- **Phone mounts:** you own both a Peak Design and a Quad Lock mount, which use different systems. Keep one and sell the other.

## 8. Repairs and building

- **Buy:** a bike multi-tool and a small screwdriver set.
- **Borrow rather than buy:** a drill and larger tools, from [Library of Things](https://www.libraryofthings.co.uk/), which rents tools from lockers across London.

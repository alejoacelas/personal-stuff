# Buying guide

Buy something only when it serves a goal or fixes a pain point below. Before buying, check the Inventory tab (including what's in Colombia). Prefer one item that solves several problems, and things that fold, stack or move easily.

## The biggest decision: sleep on a folding mattress

A trifold or futon mattress would solve three pain points at once:

- It stacks against the wall during the day, so the bed frame can go.
- It doubles as a soft surface for lying on the floor.
- Removing the frame frees the space you're short on.

To do it: buy a long ball-end hex key set (the "L-shape set") to reach the recessed bolts, disassemble the frame, then sell or donate it. Decide about the closet separately: removing it means the ~12 t-shirts and jeans need somewhere else, such as a clothes rail or two boxes under the desk.

## By goal

1. **Host house dinners.** You already have 4 small plates, 4 large plates and 4 bowls, which covers four guests. Cookware to buy: a 5 L pot and a wok. Choose one main knife: a Chinese cleaver handles vegetables, meat and scooping food into the pan; a chef's knife is easier if you rarely cut large vegetables. Add a small egg pan only if eggs in the wok annoy you. For a clean house, hire a cleaner and keep surfaces clear by owning less.
2. **Comfortable room.** A large soft rug and warm lamp light. For daylight, a large mirror beside or opposite the window reflects more sun into the room and is far easier than an outside reflector on the sixth floor.
3. **Good room for working.** A footrest (the "foot cushion"), a desk lamp, a multiplug and a paper ream.
4. **Exercise at home.** A doorframe pull-up bar that hangs on the frame without screws needs no drill, but only works on a sturdy frame with moulding; measure the door first. Gym rings hung over a door or on a park bar are the alternative. Buy two sets of exercise clothes.
5. **Move around and fix things.** For carrying shopping on the bike: a rear rack with a pannier, if your bike has rack mounts. For carrying on foot: a packable tote that lives in your backpack. For repairs: a bike multi-tool plus the hex key set; borrow a drill rather than buying one.

## Questions that change the plan

1. Does the closet stay? That decides whether you need a clothes rail.
2. Does the door frame have moulding? That decides bar versus rings.
3. Does the bike have rack mounts? That decides rack versus a larger backpack.
